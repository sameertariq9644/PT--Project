The pathway

Six steps from Pakistan to a US licence.

Here is the whole route in plain language — what each step is, what you’ll need, and where graduates most often lose time.

01 Documents

Credential evaluation

An approved agency such as FCCPT compares your DPT coursework with US physical therapy education standards. Any gaps found may need extra coursework before you can move on.

Typical time: [X–Y months]
What you’ll need
Degree and official transcripts
Course descriptions / syllabus from your university
Proof of registration or licence in Pakistan, if any
Where people get stuck: incomplete course descriptions and documents sent directly by the student instead of the university.
02 English

TOEFL iBT

Most states and credentialing bodies require TOEFL iBT scores, often with minimum scores for each section — speaking is the one most people underestimate.

Typical time: [X–Y months]
What you’ll need
Your target state’s minimum section scores
A study plan focused on speaking and writing
Scores sent directly to the right agency
Where people get stuck: booking the exam before knowing the exact section minimums for their chosen state.
03 Exam

The NPTE

The National Physical Therapy Examination, run by FSBPT, is the licensing exam every US physical therapist must pass. You need approval to test from a state board first.

Typical time: [X–Y months]
What you’ll need
Authorization to Test from your state board
A structured study plan and practice exams
A realistic exam date
Where people get stuck: attempts are limited, so testing before you’re ready is expensive in both money and time.
04 Licence

State licensure

Each state board sets its own rules for internationally educated PTs — including jurisprudence exams and, in some states, a period of supervised clinical practice.

Typical time: [X–Y months]
What you’ll need
A carefully chosen first state
Board application and fees
Jurisprudence exam, where required
Where people get stuck: picking a state for its job market without checking its rules for foreign-educated PTs.
05 Immigration

VisaScreen & visa route

Healthcare workers need a certificate (such as VisaScreen or the FCCPT certificate) before a visa can be issued. Then comes the route that fits you — for example an H-1B or an employment-based green card.

Typical time: [Varies]
What you’ll need
Healthcare Worker Certificate
A sponsoring employer for most routes
An immigration attorney for the filing itself
Where people get stuck: relying on agents who promise guaranteed visas. No one can guarantee one.
06 Arrival

Job offer & relocation

Finding employers who sponsor international PTs, presenting your experience well, and landing ready to practise from day one.

Typical time: [X–Y months]
What you’ll need
A US-style CV
Interview practice
A relocation and first-90-days plan
Where people get stuck: underselling Pakistani clinical experience in interviews.
Requirements change and differ by state. This page is a general overview; always confirm with FSBPT, FCCPT and your state board. We are not immigration lawyers — for visa filings, you’ll work with a licensed attorney.
